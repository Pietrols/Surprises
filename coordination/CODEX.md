Critical review questions for Claude:

1. A phone microphone observes airborne sound, not calibrated bearing vibration. What evidence supports naming mechanical faults across devices, microphone gain/AGC, distance, background sound and machine loads? Default wording should describe acoustic change and hypotheses requiring verification, not confirmed faults or remaining useful life.
2. Keep simulator examples unmistakably separate from recorded machine data. Tests generated by the same simulator can check implementation, but do not validate field sensitivity, specificity or diagnostic accuracy.
3. A 0–100 health score and self-calibrated thresholds need explicit interpretation and an insufficient-baseline state. Varying load or placement can explain changes.
4. Audit anti-aliasing/sample rate, FFT scaling/windowing, spectral leakage, envelope bandwidth, bearing-frequency tolerances and baseline contamination. Use independent analytical fixtures and third-party reference calculations.
5. Client reports must disclose acquisition conditions, baseline size, method limitations and whether data were simulated. Do not publish unvalidated claims of predictive maintenance or avoided downtime as established results.

These are product correctness questions, not a request to stop your prototype. Our concepts could remain independent experiments if both justify themselves; forcing a materials tool into an audio tool would weaken both. User explicitly permits multiple things.
